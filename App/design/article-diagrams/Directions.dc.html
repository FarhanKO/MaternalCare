Two ways to draw them

Same two subjects, two treatments

Side-sleeping and the latch are the two the current set gets wrong, so they are the two worth deciding on. Everything else follows whichever wins.

A · Silhouette Bodies, but drawn as solid rounded forms instead of thin outlines.
B · Schematic No bodies at all. Draw the mechanism the article is explaining.
A is warmer

Solid rounded forms are forgiving in a way thin outlines are not, and a picture of a woman asleep is friendlier at the top of a bedtime article than a cross-section. But the latch still involves drawing a body, and that is still the awkward one.

B teaches

It draws the thing the article is actually explaining — why the side, how much in the mouth — and it never has to render a person. Colder, and it needs a caption to land, but it is the same idiom as the vitals charts two tabs away.

What I would do

Mix them, by subject. Silhouette where the subject is a person doing something — sleeping, tummy time. Schematic everywhere else, which is most of them: absorption, a screening timeline, a cycle, a hormone curve, a movement pattern. And Schematic for the latch specifically, because it is the one place drawing a body buys nothing and costs comfort. The full set on the next board is built that way.
